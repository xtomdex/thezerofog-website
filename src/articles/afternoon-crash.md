# Why am I crashing at 2 PM every day?

A 2 PM crash is a normal after-lunch dip that some people get, and in a few of us it's big enough to end the working day.

For years the day had a wall in it. A good morning, then lunch, then somewhere around 2 PM the crash. The same paragraph three times. A tab open and no memory of why I opened it. By three I was pushing through on coffee, and by evening there was nothing left for anyone. Some people call it afternoon brain fog. I called it the wall.

If you typed "why am I crashing at 2 PM" into Google, you already know the moment. I typed it too, more than once, and most of what came back was either a clinic page or somebody selling a powder.

This is what I found when I stopped guessing and went looking properly. Some of it is research, some of it is what happened to me. I am not a doctor, and nothing here is medical advice.

## Is it normal to be tired after lunch?

Being tired after lunch is normal, and researchers have measured the early-afternoon dip in labs for decades.

That was the first thing that helped, because I had spent years treating it as proof that something was wrong with me, that I'm lazy or I have a discipline problem or not enough motivation.

Alertness goes up and down over the day, and in some people it dips hard in the early afternoon. Researchers call it the post-lunch dip. In the Reddit and Quora threads I read for this, the afternoon crash was the second most common complaint of the nine I counted, behind brain fog.

So the dip itself is nothing unusual. Some people get a mild version of it and push through without noticing. What was unusual was the size of mine. It did not slow the day down, it ended it, at two, with a full inbox and a head that wouldn't come back.

How I'd tell the two apart now, from my own afternoons:

| The dip | The wall |
|---|---|
| Came after lunch and lifted on its own by three | Came with or without lunch and stayed until evening |
| I'd notice it and push through | I'd read the same paragraph three times and stop |
| One coffee got me back | The second coffee did nothing |
| A slower afternoon | The day ended at two |

## Why do I crash so hard after lunch?

For me, a hard crash after lunch was mostly not the lunch. That one took me a while to believe. I had tried the light lunch, the no lunch, the salad, the protein, the walk after. One or two helped a bit, but the wall was still there at two.

In a study at the University of Pittsburgh, researchers replaced lunch with liquid feeds every hour, so there was no lunch to blame. Five of the twelve people still had the dip in the early afternoon. The authors' conclusion was that the dip is built in and that people differ in how strong it is.

What you eat can make it worse. In two studies from Sheffield, people were more tired an hour and a half after a meal than before it, and swapping a fatty meal for a high-carb one made no difference to that. In the second study a fatty meal did leave them more tired three hours later than a high-carb one. So the meal deepens a dip that was going to happen anyway.

## Should I get tested?

Getting the basic tests done is worth it, if only so you can stop wondering.

I did. Iron, thyroid, stomach. All fine. I went in hoping for a number that would explain everything, and I came out with a clean sheet and the same afternoon.

I saw the same thing again and again in the threads I read. One man on Reddit, twenty-two, listed everything he had done: two rounds of tests at a clinic, a thyroid panel, an MRI, a colonoscopy, six months without caffeine. Every result came back fine and the fatigue stayed. Hundreds of people upvoted him. My guess is most of them had the same folder of clean results.

So get the basics ruled out with a doctor, because when something is off, that's the cheap way to find it. And if the sheet comes back clean, it may just mean the tests weren't looking at the thing.

## I had a blood test that came back normal, but I still feel tired. What could be causing this?

A blood test that comes back normal rules out the things the panel was built to find, and in my case the cause wasn't on that list.

What I'd have wanted someone at the clinic to tell me about a normal result:

- **Normal is a range.** The lab calls the middle of a healthy group normal, so a number can sit right at the bottom of the band and print without a flag.
- **One morning.** Mine was drawn at eight on a good morning, and the crash was at two, when nobody draws blood.
- **Built for disease.** The panel is there to find a disease, and in my case it did its job, there wasn't one. There's no line on it for a day that ends at two.

Every fix I tried for years assumed the problem was me and I wasn't using the day properly. Get disciplined, or get a better system, or a better morning. They were all the same fix with a different name.

None of them could have worked, and it had nothing to do with willpower or motivation. One thing had been wrong the whole time, and none of the tests were looking at it. But it's not something exotic, there's nothing magical about it, and it isn't genetics either. It's something you can measure, and it runs out. When it's already empty, the day is decided before you open the laptop.

In my case, that's what a clean test sheet and a 2 PM crash meant together. If the tank is full you get a dip. If it's empty you get the wall. Standard blood tests don't measure what fills it.

## Two things that helped before I understood any of it

Two things made my afternoons easier years before I knew what was going on. Neither fixed the crash, but I still do both.

Ten minutes outside at the first sign of the wall. Not a walk to think, just out the door and back. It beat the second coffee every time (and the second coffee wasn't doing anything anyway).

The hard task before the wall, never after. The morning was the only part of my day that was ever reliable, so the one thing that had to move forward went there, first, before email. Everything that only needed hands went to the afternoon. It didn't cure anything, but a bad afternoon stopped costing me the whole day.

## Questions people ask about the afternoon crash

The other questions Google puts next to mine, answered short.

### What causes you to crash in the afternoon?

In the research, a crash in the afternoon comes from a dip that is built into some people, and a meal makes it deeper. Lunch gets the blame first, and when it isn't the lunch, most people blame themselves. I did, for years, with a clean set of tests in the drawer.

### Why do I crash out at 3pm?

A crash at 3pm is the same crash an hour later. In the threads I read for this the time ran from one to six in the afternoon, and two was the most common. Mine was at two, and the threads had plenty of threes.

### How do I stop crashing in the afternoon?

I couldn't stop it with anything on the usual lists: the lighter lunch, the walk, the second coffee, the better morning. Some of them made an afternoon easier. The crash only went when I found the actual cause, and that wasn't on any of those lists.

### What is an afternoon slump?

An afternoon slump is the early-afternoon dip in alertness that researchers call the post-lunch dip. Some people get a mild version and push through it. Mine was big enough to end the day, which is why I never called it a slump.
