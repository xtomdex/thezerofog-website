# I had a blood test that came back normal, but I still feel tired. What could be causing this?

A normal blood test rules out the few diseases the panel was built to catch. In three studies of people who went to the doctor tired, a disease turned out to be the reason in about 8 of every 100.

I had that sheet. Iron, thyroid, stomach, all fine, and the afternoon hadn't changed. For a while a clean result made it worse. If nothing was wrong with me, then the wall at two was my fault, and I'd believed that for years.

Before the tests I'd tried productivity apps and supplements and a better morning. I was trying to find the cause outside myself.

I'm not a doctor and none of this is medical advice. Nobody at the clinic showed me these numbers, so here they are next to what happened to me.

## Why am I so tired if my blood tests are normal?

Most people who go to the doctor tired come out with a normal panel, because the panel looks for a short list of diseases and the tiredness is usually not on it.

What the studies I found actually say:

- **Netherlands, 325 people.** New, unexplained tiredness, sent for blood tests, and the tests found a disease in eight per cent. The wider panel added more false alarms.
- **Netherlands, 571 people.** Followed for a year, and half of them never got any diagnosis that explained the tiredness. Clear disease in 8 per cent.
- **England and Wales, 220 people.** Four practices. The lab results contributed to a diagnosis in 19 of them.
- **Israel, 299 people aged 18 to 45.** Most were sent for blood tests, 5 per cent of those tests came back positive, and about a quarter of the people eventually got a specific diagnosis, mostly anaemia or an infection.

Nobody gave me any of those numbers. The doctor said everything looked fine, and I went home hearing that the problem was me.

I kept seeing the same story in the threads. One was a 21-year-old student, fit, decent habits, normal bloods, so tired and foggy he couldn't study for his exams. His doctor said depression. He said he'd got tired first and depressed afterwards. Dozens of replies, and the ones people upvoted each had a different diagnosis for him, or a specialist he should go and see.

## Can blood work be normal and still have something wrong?

Blood work can be normal with something still wrong, because normal on the report only means you landed inside the middle 95 per cent of a healthy group.

The way a lab sets a normal range, as far as I can read it, is to take at least 120 healthy people, measure them, and call the middle 95 per cent normal. The edges of that band are still normal. So a number right at the bottom of the range prints without a flag and looks the same as one in the middle. And by the same arithmetic, one healthy person in twenty falls outside the range on any single test, which is part of why the wider panel in the Dutch trial added false alarms.

None of that makes the panel useless. It's very good at what it's for, which is finding a disease. In my case it did its job, and there wasn't one. It can't tell you why a day that started fine ended at two. There's no line on the sheet for that.

## What a clean sheet meant in my case

In my case the clean sheet and the crash at two in the afternoon had one cause, and no test on that sheet looks at it.

I wasn't unusual. In the Reddit and Quora threads I read for this, "did everything right and still wrecked" was the third most common complaint of the nine I counted.

Every fix I tried assumed I was the problem. More discipline, a new system, a supplement. None of them could have worked, because what I was short of isn't a character trait. It's nothing rare, it isn't genetic, and a standard panel doesn't look at it.

## Two things I'd tell that student

I'd tell that student two things: get the basics ruled out with a doctor, once, and then treat a clean sheet as information about what it isn't, not as a mark against you.

Get the basics ruled out, because when something is off, the panel is the cheap way to find it. When it comes back clean, that's information too. In my case it meant the thing behind my afternoons wasn't on that list. Ordering more tests on my own would have been one more fix aimed at the wrong place, and the Dutch trial is the reason I'd give now: the wider panel added more false alarms.

A clean sheet isn't a verdict that you're lazy. I read mine that way for years and it cost me more than the tiredness did. The tests only ask whether something is broken. Mine said no. That wasn't what I needed to know.

## Where I ended up

I stopped looking for the cause outside myself. I stopped listening to gurus and stuck to doctors who see patients all day and say what they actually see. What I found had nothing clever about it. I work full days now, and there's still something left in the evening for the people close to me.
